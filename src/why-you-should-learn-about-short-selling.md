# Why You Should Learn About Short Selling

Should you learn about short selling? Most definitely. Short selling and *knowing about* short selling are two different matters — so even if you don't wish to engage in the practice (understandable, given its drawbacks), understanding the approach of a short seller will help you develop a well-rounded skill set as an investor or trader. Here are some reasons why nurturing your inner bear is time well spent.

## It will improve your research skills

Given the limited upside and unlimited downside to shorting, short sellers are very thorough with their due diligence. They spend hours going through filings, news articles and research papers, and carefully researching the backgrounds of the people involved with publicly listed companies. This is time-consuming, which is why short sellers develop techniques that increase the quality of their research while reducing the time it takes.

## It will improve your trading skills

Timing is everything to a short seller. Short too early and you lose money; too late, thanks to the uptick rule, and you miss the move entirely. As a result, short sellers need a solid foundation in technical analysis — price action, volume fluctuations and key indicators all need to be well understood to devise a successful strategy.

## It will thicken your skin

Short sellers aren't popular; they're contrarians by nature. Expressing a negative view of a company will often draw a fair amount of vitriol from current shareholders, sometimes even management. This teaches you to deal with detractors and abuse calmly — keeping a cool head matters in the highly volatile, often emotional world of trading.

## It will improve your critical thinking skills

Questioning and assessing the nature and value of information is paramount for well-informed decisions. Short sellers are sceptical by nature, always on the lookout for information that counters the dominant bullish narrative. Understanding a short seller's approach provides insight into a frame of mind built on real critical thinking.

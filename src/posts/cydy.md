<p>Over the last seven weeks CYDY’s share price has a seen a near five-fold increase, daily trading volume has increased drastically and daily RSI (14) breached 80 several times. <h4 data-fontsize="18" data-lineheight="19">Company information:</h4>
<p>CytoDyn Inc. was originally incorporated in Colorado in May 2002 under the name RexRay Corporation. It describes itself as “a late-stage biotechnology company focused on the clinical development and potential commercialization of leronlimab (PRO 140), a CCR5 antagonist to treat HIV infection, with the potential for multiple therapeutic indications.” Leronlimab has been around for a few years. It obtained FDA fast track designation in 2006 and completed its first clinical trials in 2007. It was acquired by CYDY in July, 2012 and is yet to gain FDA approval.</p>
<p>The beginning of CYDY recent share price run coincided with news of the company signing a definitive agreement with Vyera Pharmaceuticals to commercialize Leronlimab in the U.S. for the treatment of HIV. News relating to Leronlimab “promising” metastatic breast cancer Phase 1b/2 trial data resulted in further price gains despite the data being limited to two patients. And more recently it has been swept up in the Corona virus induced bio craze that has resulted in many dubious tickers exhibiting highly suspect price runs.</p>

<h4>Questions over management</h4>
<p>CYDY has been no stranger to litigation over the last few years. In 2018 it was sued by an institutional shareholder due to the fact that members of CytoDyn’s Board of Directors (the “Board”) and the Board’s Compensation Committee breached their fiduciary duties by awarding excessive stock options to Nader Z. Pourhassan, CytoDyn’s CEO. It turned out that Mr. Pourhassan had obtained a total of 1,604,545 options which greatly exceeded the 1,000,000 cap per individual. The company had no choice but to settle by rescinding 604,545 of the CEO’s shares ( <a href="https://content.equisolve.net/cytodyn/media/806dae129baedd6ce4320374ebfdcf96.pdf">1</a> ).</p>

<p><strong>A very dodgy creditor:</strong></p>
<p>CYDY also relies on debt to stay afloat. And one of its creditors has made been mentioned in more than one of our reports due to its highly dubious lending practices. This entity is Iliad and Research trading. In our INPX short report which was published on January 6, 2020, we highlighted how Iliad along with its affiliates entered into note purchase agreements that allowed them to obtain over 65 million shares of INPX common stock during the last three months of 2019 at an average price of less than $0.06 per share. INPX then went on to experience a seventeen-fold price increase followed by an 87% decrease over a three-week period. For more details here is a <a href="/2020/01/06/inpx/">link to said short report</a>. Iliad’s involvement as a creditor is thus a very bad omen.</p>
<p>There are other instances when Iliad has been involved with suspect tickers. According to our Asgard database (a proprietary database and stock selection tool), these tickers include: CV Sciences (CVSI), Netlist (NLST) and NovaBay Pharma (NBY).&nbsp; These tickers have experienced long term decreases in their share price characterised by high levels of volatility.</p>
<p>CYDY owes Iliad Research and trading a fair amount of money, to be precise $10.4 million. This debt was issued through two separate $5.7 million convertible promissory notes dated January 30, 2019 ( <a href="https://www.otcmarkets.com/filing/html?id=13179861&amp;guid=d_qjU6R4Lc2zZth">6</a> ) and June 26, 2018 ( <a href="https://www.otcmarkets.com/filing/html?id=12832397&amp;guid=QKwCUp1NXQ82k3h">7</a> ).</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list"><li>Yesterday, Eros’s key Indian operating subsidiary had its credit rating lowered 10 notches to “default” by CARE ratings, the second largest Indian ratings agency. The issue, according to CARE was “a slowdown in collection from debtors”.</li><li>The default occurred just as we were completing an investigation that sought to explain precisely why Eros has been persistently unable to collect receivables from its debtors.</li><li>After extensive on-the-ground research in India, interviews with multiple former employees, and a detailed review of Indian private company filings, we believe the underlying problem is that a significant portion of Eros’s receivables don’t actually exist.</li><li>We have uncovered details of highly irregular related-party transactions. For example, Eros has directed $153 million to a supposed production company based in tiny office located in what looks to be a rehabilitated Mumbai slum. The entity is operated by the brother-in-law of Eros’s Chairman and CEO.</li><li>We have also documented what we believe to be multiple undisclosed related-party transactions that appear designed to hide receivables.</li><li>It is hard to imagine Eros’s equity makes it out of this scenario intact. We expect the price of both the BSE and NYSE stock to end up worthless, barring some sort of bailout from a friend of Eros’s leadership.</li><li>In our opinion, this situation has arisen due to a complete failure of Eros’s auditor, Grant Thornton, to apply even basic scrutiny to Eros’s financials.</li></ul>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Introduction: The Market Was Caught Off-Guard by the Sudden Credit Default of Eros’s Key Operating Subsidiary</strong></h3>



<p>The last couple of days has seen a dramatic series of events for Eros, a company that has been dogged by allegations of accounting irregularities for years (including in several articles written by us:&nbsp;<a href="https://hindenburgresearch.com/eros-earnings-review-an-abundance-of-red-flags/">1</a>,&nbsp;<a href="https://hindenburgresearch.com/eros-international-new-receivables-accounting-red-flags/">2</a>).</p>



<p>India’s second largest credit ratings agency,&nbsp;<a href="http://www.careratings.com/findratings.aspx?cid=bniHSqlzInX2Sp%2f0M2lulQ%3d%3d">CARE ratings</a>, suddenly lowered the rating on Eros’s key Indian operating subsidiary by&nbsp;<a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-06-06/bonds-rally-in-india-after-central-bank-signals-more-rate-cuts">10 notches</a>&nbsp;to “Default”, catalyzing a wave of intense selling pressure across Eros’s entire capital structure.</p>



<p>Eros’s Bombay Stock Exchange-listed subsidiary subsequently&nbsp;<a href="https://www.bseindia.com/stock-share-price/eros-international-media-ltd/erosmedia/533261/">cratered</a>&nbsp;limit-down, along with Eros’s&nbsp;<a href="https://www.londonstockexchange.com/exchange/prices-and-markets/retail-bonds/company-summary/XS1112834608ZZGBPUKCP.html">unsecured bonds</a>&nbsp;trading -43%, followed by its NYSE-listed stock which finished the day down about 50%.</p>



<p>Eros initially responded to the default news with a morning&nbsp;<a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20190606005449/en/Eros-International-PLC-Issues-Statement-Announces-Fiscal">press release</a>&nbsp;stating:</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow"><p>Eros International PLC and all of its subsidiaries have met and continue to meet all debt service commitments.</p></blockquote>



<p>The story already shifted by mid-day however when a follow-up&nbsp;<a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20190606005781/en/Eros-International-PLC-Issues-Clarifying-Statement">press release</a>&nbsp;acknowledged that its subsidiary was actually late on two loan payments:</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow"><p>(the subsidiary) was late on two loan interest payments for April and May 2019. These interest payments total less than $2 million and are currently in process of remittance.</p></blockquote>



<p>The release then reiterated the company’s strong cash and liquidity position, although this seemed to do little to allay investor concerns (the stock remained roughly flat from the time of announcement until end of day).</p>



<p>The implosion of Eros’s capital structure seems to have caught many market participants off-guard. Macquarie analysts covering the stock put out a research note entitled “<a href="http://view.remail.macquarie.com/?qs=eb2e24c33cff0a43b851ad1f2aab03d9987e487fcaf916adb9a093313104bef86278adc418df1b25756de3dc5383e36997cce78044adc7e6f2657aa51743f2d10652a0250709dde7c6c7ca5c819b2a55">Hard to Explain</a>”, with their best guess being that Eros defaulted due to a “clerical error”.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Introduction: It’s Not Hard to Explain</strong></h3>



<p>For us, this unraveling couldn’t have been more expected. We have been tracking Eros’s rapidly deteriorating financial condition closely and were in the process of finalizing this investigation which sought to understand these very issues in more depth.</p>



<p>With Eros’s high short-term debt balance, its&nbsp;<a href="http://view.remail.macquarie.com/?qs=eb2e24c33cff0a43b851ad1f2aab03d9987e487fcaf916adb9a093313104bef86278adc418df1b25756de3dc5383e36997cce78044adc7e6f2657aa51743f2d10652a0250709dde7c6c7ca5c819b2a55">recent failure to raise an onshore bond</a>, and its failure to collect on its suspiciously high receivables balance, a liquidity event seemed to border on inevitable.</p>

<p>The reasoning cited by CARE for the default rating was a “slowdown in collection from debtors” which led to cash flow issues at the company. Per the&nbsp;<a href="http://www.careratings.com/upload/CompanyFiles/PR/Eros%20International%20Media%20Limited-06-05-2019.pdf">report synopsis</a>:</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/1.png" alt="" class="wp-image-406"></figure>



<p>The “slowdown in collection” echoes the criticism of multiple short-sellers over the past several years (including us) who had identified patterns of suspicious related-party entities and large amounts of revenue that the company has had persistent difficulty in collecting.</p>



<p>Quite frankly, our belief is that Eros is having a hard time collecting on its receivables and advances because we think a significant portion of them do not actually exist.</p>



<p>When faced with past questions about revenue and receivables from skeptics Eros chose to&nbsp;<a href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20171003005903/en/Eros-International-Plc-Commences-Legal-Action-Market">sue them all</a>, including us, alleging a massive “short and distort” conspiracy. The lawsuit was recently&nbsp;<a href="https://iapps.courts.state.ny.us/fbem/DocumentDisplayServlet?documentId=e6dTaZ/5upgM8E3xnAbL/Q==&amp;system=prod">dismissed</a>&nbsp;against all moving defendants.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>About Our Investigation: On the Ground in India, And Multiple Interviews with Former Employees</strong></h3>



<p>Instead of cowering to Eros’s bullying tactics, we instead decided to take our research to the next level.</p>



<p>In this report, we will share findings from our on-the-ground investigation to Mumbai, and from interviews conducted with multiple former employees globally.</p>



<p>Our primary goal was to learn more about Eros’s mysterious and growing uncollected receivables balance, which appears to be at the core of its current liquidity situation.</p>



<p>As we will show, we think Eros’s collapse is an egregious failure of its auditors, Grant Thornton, to apply even basic scrutiny to the company’s financial condition. This comes as no surprise: In the wake of another ‘mysterious’ financial collapse at one of its other clients last year, the&nbsp;<a href="https://www.ft.com/content/f8828dc8-24b0-11e9-8ce6-5db4543da632">CEO of Grant Thornton said</a>&nbsp;(quite infamously):</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow"><p>We are not doing what the market thinks. We are not looking for fraud and we are not looking at the future and we are not giving a statement that the accounts are correct…we are not set up to look for fraud.</p></blockquote>



<p>You can say that again Grant Thornton.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Next Gen: A High-End Office Fit for a Major Bollywood Production Studio? No—A Small Office Based in a Mumbai Rehabilitated Slum?</strong></h3>



<p>We visited Next Gen’s offices and found it to be far from what you would expect out of a supposed high-end Bollywood production company allocating hundreds of millions of dollars to movie projects.</p>



<p>Next Gen’s&nbsp;<a href="http://nextgenfilms.in/about-ngf/">website</a>&nbsp;doesn’t list an address, so we had to dig through the entity’s Indian&nbsp;<a href="https://www.slideshare.net/secret/dAorZDokH4g0Nc">private company filings</a>&nbsp;to find its headquarters. The address is listed as ‘6-A/10, Junu Sangeeta Apartment, Santacruz (West), Mumbai.’</p>



<p>The offices are located in the Juhu Beach area and, more precisely, in a small enclosed area called “Sangeeta Apartments”. As the name suggests, and unsurprisingly for the location of a suspected shell company, the area is largely residential. Here we are at the Sangeeta Apartment complex on May 4<sup>th</sup>&nbsp;of this year:</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/5-768x1024.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-410"></figure>



<p>And here is the Indian investigator in front of Next Gen’s offices (you can see the Next Gen sign in the background)<a href="https://hindenburgresearch.com/eros-international-on-the-ground-research-employee-interviews-and-private-company-documents-expose-egregious-accounting-irregularities/#_ftn1">[1]</a>:</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/6-769x1024.png" alt="" class="wp-image-411"></figure>



<p>The company is located on the ground floor of what has been identified by one of our Indian advisors as an SRA building. SRA stands for “slum rehabilitation authority”, a government institution in charge of taking slum constructions and, with minimal modifications, turning them into buildings safe for living. This is clear from the photograph, which shows metal bars at the exterior of the building — typical signs of SRA intervention.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Our Indian consultant explained that many “dodgy” businesses tend to be located in slums or former slum locations because the authorities are less likely to check the premises and interfere with their activities.</p>



<p>We were able to briefly enter the Next Gen offices and accessed what seemed to be the main area. It was a small room with a desk and a couple of posters. We saw little to no commercial activity going on at the premises.</p>



<p>Immediately upon entrance, a middle-aged man with a mustache approached us with a worried and suspicious disposition. This person, as far as we could tell, was the only employee present during our three visits to the premises, and was unable to communicate in English.&nbsp;</p>



<p>The impression we got from this visit is that of a sham office where little to no real activity is going on at a location totally unsuitable for movie production; an industry most would associate with a nice building, well-stocked offices, and plenty of high-level employees.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Next Gen: Former Employee Claimed That Next Gen Channels Money to Lulla Family Members Through Dummy Production Deals</strong></h3>



<p>A 2016 class action lawsuit against Eros included witness testimony alleging that Eros uses Next Gen as a means of funneling money to the company’s insiders. Per the amended complaint [<a href="https://www.slideshare.net/secret/F4RQhhPoly8Opg">Pg. 22</a>]:</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow"><p>Confidential Witness (“CW”) 2 is an Indian film producer with personal knowledge of the Company’s business practices in the period immediately leading up to the Class Period, as a result of his work co-producing films with Eros, including at least one film released in 2011. According to CW2,&nbsp;<strong>Eros channels money to family members through dummy production deals</strong>.</p><p>According to CW2, 30-40% of Eros’s acquisition and production occurs through Next Gen, owned by Kishore Lulla’s brother-in-law Puja Rajami. Further, according to CW2, NextGen signs the co-production agreements with EIM, and Eros makes payments to EIM, which, in turn, makes payments to NextGen to produce the films.</p><p><strong>Thus, NextGen collects money on films but provides no added value, according to CW2</strong>. CW2 also avers that&nbsp;<strong>NextGen employees use Eros’s offices and do little other than make a margin on the film</strong>. CW2 also avers that Lulla’s wife also receives advances as a producer.</p></blockquote>



<p>The lawsuit was dismissed, likely due to a lack of additional evidence. In light of our latest findings on Next Gen, however, we view the witness testimony as highly prescient.</p>

<p>As we see from its latest&nbsp;<a href="https://www.slideshare.net/secret/bb6AgK0qBKmru0">audit</a>, Eros Television has generated zero operating revenue for the prior 2 years, had zero inventory, no fixed assets, and no investments, yet its balance sheet is laden with large, suspicious advances and payables:</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Eros Television: Did Eros Fail to Disclose Related Party Transactions with This Entity… Again?</strong></h3>



<p>As we had noted earlier, Eros sued us in 2017 after&nbsp;<a href="https://iapps.courts.state.ny.us/fbem/DocumentDisplayServlet?documentId=6n5DXfvi3_PLUS_p71JT9ogi44Q==&amp;system=prod">we had identified</a>&nbsp;that the company appeared to be engaging in undisclosed related-party transactions. We had initially emailed Eros to ask about such transactions, but rather than respond to our questions they instead filed a lawsuit against us and dozens of others in an effort to silence criticism.</p>



<p>Despite the lawsuit, Eros eventually admitted in its 2017 financials that it had taken a $6.4 million loan from Eros Television, one of the very entities we had identified as suspicious. [<a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1532981/000117152017000332/eps7429.htm">Pg. 98</a>]</p>



<p>While we were pleased to see that Eros eventually disclosed its dealings with Eros Television in its 2017 financials, we see that no related party transactions with Eros Television were disclosed in&nbsp;<a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1532981/000117152018000361/eps8065.htm">2018</a>:</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/17.png" alt="" class="wp-image-421"></figure>



<p>(Note that Eros Television was mentioned 3 other times in the&nbsp;<a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1532981/000117152018000361/eps8065.htm">2018 financials</a>, but similarly disclosed no other transactions aside from the 2017 transaction.)</p>



<p>We found this incredibly surprising. We see that Eros Television in fact engaged in&nbsp;<em>plenty</em>&nbsp;of related party transactions in 2018 according to the entity’s own&nbsp;<a href="https://www.slideshare.net/secret/bb6AgK0qBKmru0">audit</a>:</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/18.png" alt="" class="wp-image-422"></figure>



<p>As you can see from the above, the large transfers have gone through this entity both to and from Eros’s subsidiaries and its key individuals. &nbsp;Aside from this apparent system of advances and payables we were unable to identify any other operations or purpose for this entity.</p>



<p>We view this as another major failure of Eros’s auditor, Grant Thornton, to monitor an entity with a documented history of engaging in undisclosed related-party transactions. There is simply no excuse for this repeated failure.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Eros Television: Our Visit to The Entity’s Auditor, Based Out of a Tiny Office in A Dilapidated Building</strong></h3>

<p>We see from Eros Television’s&nbsp;<a href="https://www.slideshare.net/secret/bb6AgK0qBKmru0">latest audit</a>&nbsp;that the entity is audited by a firm called R.R. Gawande &amp; Co. The proprietor can be reached by email at either his Hotmail account or his Yahoo account:</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/19.png" alt="" class="wp-image-423"></figure>



<p>We visited Gawande’s offices in May 2019.&nbsp;The office is located in a humble neighborhood in Mumbai called Goregaon and within a commercial building called “Express Zone”. To enter we first walked past a few cows lounging under a tent at the gate:</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/20-768x1024.jpg" alt="" class="wp-image-424"></figure>



<p>On the inside, the facility was poorly-lit and run-down. Most of the commercial space looked vacant.&nbsp;</p>



<p>Gawande’s office was clearly marked with a sign on the second floor. The office was tiny, barely enough for a single desk and a table with a mug of coffee.&nbsp;</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/image.png" alt="" class="wp-image-433"></figure>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What About ErosNow? Usage Metrics Show That the Product is a Dud</strong></h3>



<p>Speaking of ‘now’, the equity bull case for Eros has generally relied on a belief that ErosNow would be a massive success. In a liquidation scenario, creditors would also want to know what the value of the platform could be.</p>



<p>In its last&nbsp;<a href="https://erosplc.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/eros-international-plc-reports-third-quarter-fy-2019-results">earnings release</a>, Eros touted that ErosNow reached 15.9 million paying subscribers, an impressive milestone.</p>



<p>Despite the rosy headline, Eros’s accounting around ErosNow revenue looks muddled. Rather than simply reporting revenue from the platform, last quarter’s release (for example) just vaguely declared that revenue growth was “fueled primarily by Eros Now” without stating clearly how much was generated from the platform.</p>



<p>Understanding true usage and adoption is also a challenge based on Eros’s reported numbers. The “paying subscribers” metric, for example, seems to consist largely of users that have simply bought new phones that come with pre-paid content bundles. Such numbers say nothing about usage or user engagement.</p>



<p>The platform’s active users are minimal, according to independent metrics provided by KalaGato, a service regularly cited by Indian media that tracks over-the-top (OTT) content app usage and downloads.</p>



<p>According to KalaGato, ErosNow barely registers as a player in the space, ranking 15<sup>th</sup>&nbsp;with a market share of about 0.16% as of December 2018.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/22.png" alt="" class="wp-image-426"><figcaption>(Source: KalaGato)</figcaption></figure>



<p>Google Trends similarly shows that the ErosNow&nbsp;<a href="https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%205-y&amp;geo=IN&amp;q=%2Fg%2F11bzzydgcy">topic</a>&nbsp;has basically flatlined in India since mid-2015, a time period that corresponds to their&nbsp;<a href="https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/indias-erosnow-originals-netflix-launch-809400">initial marketing campaign</a>&nbsp;to roll out the platform:</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image"><img decoding="async" src="https://hindenburgresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/23.png" alt="" class="wp-image-427"><figcaption>Source: Google Trends</figcaption></figure>



<p>By all measures ErosNow’s usage seems minimal, despite years of company suggestions that the platform represents the future of Eros.</p>

<ol class="wp-block-list"><li>The auditors clearly looked the other way despite years of warning signs.</li><li>The bankers seemed more interested in generating fees through debt &amp; equity offerings than in performing credible underwriting.</li><li>The complex international structure made it too challenging for regulators to enforce.</li></ol>
